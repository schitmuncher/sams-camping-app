I'm making a small button-based text survival game called "Pitch Dark" (working title). You play one night wild camping in the UK with your real camping kit. I'd like your help as a writer. Please read all of this first, then work through the TASKS in order, one batch at a time, and wait for me to say "next" between batches.

THE GAME IN BRIEF
- Each scene is 2–4 short lines of text and 2–4 tap buttons. It's played on a phone, one-handed.
- Five meters (0–100): warmth, energy, dry, morale, suspicion (suspicion is danger: 100 means you get asked to leave).
- Phases: walk (walking in, 30-min turns), pitch (choosing a spot and putting up the shelter before sunset), camp (evening), night (1-hour turns, roughly 17:00–07:00 in winter), dawn (packing up).
- Nobody dies. Bad nights end with: "bailed" (too cold, walk home), "movedon" (spotted and asked to leave), "calledit" (morale gone), "exhausted" (energy gone on the walk).
- The player's real kit decides what's possible. No lighter means the stove won't light. Wrong pegs won't go into frozen ground. A flask means a hot drink at 3am.
- First scenario: a November night by the Shropshire Union / Llangollen canals near Hurleston and Nantwich, Cheshire. Around −1°C, 85% humidity, dark by 16:30, sunrise about 08:00.

TONE
Mostly straight, quietly tense and atmospheric, with occasional dry British humour in small doses ("The cows are judging you."). Second person, present tense ("You wake shivering."). Plain words, no purple prose. Be accurate about real wild camping (condensation, cold ground, frost, pegs, Leave No Trace, being considerate to landowners). Don't encourage trespass or ignoring "no camping" requests. Stealth means not disturbing anyone. Only use real local places you're confident exist. If unsure, keep it generic ("the lock", "the farm lane").

TEXT PLACEHOLDERS YOU CAN USE (the game fills them with the player's real kit)

RULES FOR GOOD STORYLETS
- text: 1–3 sentences, under 220 characters. label: under 40 characters. result: 1–2 sentences, under 160 characters.
- Every storylet needs at least one choice with no "requires", so the player is never stuck.
- Kit should matter: give a better option when the right kit is present (requires hasItem), and sometimes a worse one when it's missing.
- Choices should be real trade-offs (e.g. warmer but noisier, faster but riskier, drier but costs energy).
- Mix bad, neutral and good moments. Owls, stars and sunrise are rewards.
